Weekly Data-Visualization Projects

I'm a UC Berkeley student taking DigHum 150B this summer, a class about data visualization for the humanities. Each week I pick one of the visualization tools we're learning and use it to look at a humanities topic I'm curious about. This site is where I post that work. I built it myself in HTML and CSS instead of using a site builder.

The weekly project

The assignment

From Week 2 to Week 5, choose one data visualization tool you learn in the week and do a visual analysis on a humanities topic you like (broadly construed). Due 11:59 PM on Sunday every week. Post your work on your site.

So every week has two moving parts: a tool and a topic. The tool is whatever we covered that week; the topic is something from the humanities that I actually want to poke at with it. Below is the running list. Each page walks through what the tool does, the data I used, and what the visualization helped me see.

Final project

Is the world eating the same meal? Sixty years of global food data say yes, increasingly, and they say whose meal it is: national diets have converged for six decades toward the plate wealthy Western countries were already eating in 1961, while the gap in how much people eat has narrowed far less. One plate, unequal portions.
